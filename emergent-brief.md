# SeanBuilds website rebuild brief (for emergent.sh) — v2

**This supersedes the v1 brief.** v1 said "stay dark." Scrap that — the new references are light-mode, and that's the direction now.

## Who this is for

SeanBuilds is a solo dev/automation studio. I build automations, custom internal tools, AI integrations, and compliance/security systems (I have a security + AI background) for small businesses, founders, and growing teams who can't justify a big dev agency. No enterprise pitch, no agency fluff. I want people to land on this site and think "this guy already knows the answer," not "this is a template with my name on it."

## The references — match these closely

- **https://shakuro.com/** — off-white/cream background, near-black ink text, almost no color at all beyond real client logos. Huge confident bold sans-serif headlines. Content separated by thin hairline dividers, not bordered cards. Real case-study entries: a device-mockup photo (laptop/phone render) paired directly with a client testimonial quote and named attribution. A full-color real logo wall (Google, TBWA, Universal, etc). A 3D abstract chrome sculpture as a brand visual centerpiece. A small persistent circular rotating badge + chat bubble in the bottom corner ("talk to an expert / free consult") that follows scroll. Award badges row (Clutch, Awwwards, Behance style).
- **https://phenomenonstudio.com/service/web-app-design/** — same light off-white/near-black base, plus one accent color (orange) used sparingly: CTA buttons, bullet markers, link underlines. Smooth organic "blob"-shaped full-bleed black section dividers breaking up the white sections (curved top edge, not a hard rectangle cut). A repeating two-column editorial row pattern: short label on the left ("Stakeholder interview," "Testing & validation"), bigger headline + body + bullet list on the right, each row separated by a hairline. Video-testimonial grid near the bottom.

This is the direction now: **light, editorial, confident, near-monochrome.** Generous whitespace. No dark mode.

## Color

Base: off-white background, near-black ink (not pure white/pure black, slightly warm-neutral like the references). One locked accent color used sparingly for CTAs and small marks, the same way Phenomenon uses orange. My existing brand accent is a signal-blue (see the wordmark "SeanBuilds </>" — blue on "Builds"). Default to keeping that blue as the one accent unless it looks wrong against this light palette, in which case orange or a fully monochrome black/white treatment (like Shakuro) are both fine fallbacks. Show me a couple of options if you can.

## Layout devices to use

- Hairline dividers between content blocks instead of bordered/shadowed cards.
- A repeating editorial row pattern for the process/approach section: small label left, big headline + body right, separated by thin rules.
- Organic blob-shaped section transitions where the background flips from light to dark (or back), not a hard rectangular cut.
- Real "case study" style entries pairing a project visual with a short testimonial and named attribution underneath, if/when I have real work to show. Right now I don't have client-ready case studies, so placeholder this section honestly (clearly marked as coming soon) rather than inventing fake projects or quotes.
- A persistent small interactive corner element (circular rotating badge + chat bubble) that links to booking a call, present on scroll throughout.

## What to keep from the current build (still true regardless of light/dark)

- The hero idea: an animated node/graph or abstract 3D object representing "untangling a system." I like this concept (the Shakuro chrome-sculpture device is a good model for how to render it with more polish).
- The signature line: **"If it takes fourteen clicks to get to a solution, that's not you. That's the system."** Build a real bold-typography moment around this, similar to Phenomenon's huge headline rows.
- Site IA: Home, What I Build, Blog, FAQ (with a contact/book-a-call destination).
- One CTA label per intent, used identically everywhere: "Book a Call" for contact, "See the Work" for portfolio. No synonyms scattered around.

## Voice

Direct, no fluff, short sentences, confident without being arrogant. No corporate buzzwords ("elevate," "seamless," "unleash," "revolutionize"). I'd rather sound like a sharp operator talking to another business owner than a SaaS landing page or generic agency.

## What I build (content to work with)

- **Automation** — approvals, reporting, manual handoffs that run themselves.
- **Custom internal tools** — built around how the team actually works, not a generic template.
- **AI, used right** — applied where it earns its place, not because it's trendy.
- **Compliance & security** — CMMC-grade thinking applied to systems, not just paperwork (this is the differentiator, I run a separate compliance product called Sentinel CMMC and bring that lens to every build).

Bio facts: 20+ projects shipped, 8+ years in security and software, thousands of hours building. No real client logos or case studies yet, don't invent any.

## Hard no's

- No generic AI-slop tells: no purple gradients, no identical three-card rows, no fake div-based product screenshots, no em-dashes, no "Quietly trusted by" style copy, no section-number eyebrows, no version-label badges.
- Don't invent client logos, testimonials, video clips, or fake-precise stats. If a section needs real social proof I don't have yet, mark it as a placeholder rather than fabricating it.

## Deliverable

A site that looks and feels like Shakuro/Phenomenon's quality bar: light, confident, editorial, real motion on scroll (organic section transitions, reveal-on-scroll text, hover detail on project imagery), something I'd actually be proud to send a prospect.
